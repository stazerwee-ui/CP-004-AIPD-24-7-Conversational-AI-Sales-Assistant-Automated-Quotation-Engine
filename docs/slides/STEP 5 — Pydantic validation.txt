STEP 7 — GST and rounding

"So once the rules engine has totalled everything up, 9% GST is applied."

Python's built-in round() rounds halves to the nearest even number — so 2.5 becomes 2 instead of 3,  and 4.5 becomes 4 instead of 5. That's not how you'd round money.

And this isn't a rare edge case for us — every price in our catalog is a round hundred, so multiplying by 0.09 often lands exactly on a half.

So we wrote a small function that rounds the normal way. It's ordinary Python and runs on the server, so the browser receives an already-rounded figure."

STEP 8 — The answer travels back

"Okay, so at this point the calculation's done — we've got the subtotal, the tax, and the total, plus the list of line items.

Now we need to send all that back to the browser. These two classes describe what the response looks like. The first one, BreakdownItem, is just one line — a name, a price, and a type. The second one, PricingResponse, is the whole reply: the three numbers plus a list of those items.

So the return line packs everything into one object, and FastAPI converts it back into text and sends it over port 8000.

And the thing worth pointing out — we're not just sending back one number. Every line comes back with its name and price. That's what lets the planner show the full itemised breakdown instead of just a total."
